A pattern that pours fine but sits crooked in the cup is its own kind of frustrating — the technique worked, and the result still looks wrong.

Symmetry problems almost always come from one of three places: where you aimed, how you held the cup, or an uneven stream. Each has a different fix, so start by working out which one you have.

## Diagnose from the finished cup

Look straight down at the design before you take a sip:

- **The whole pattern sits to one side of the cup** → aim problem. The pour point was off the centre line.
- **The pattern is centred but twisted, or one side is bigger** → cup handling problem. The cup rotated or tilted mid-pour.
- **The pattern drifts sideways as it grows, especially in a wiggle pattern** → flow problem. The stream is not falling straight.

Milk texture can make any of these look worse, but it rarely causes asymmetry by itself. If the milk was glossy, the cause is in the hands.

## Aim: pour onto a line, not a point

Symmetry starts before the pattern does. When you set the base, picture a line running down the middle of the cup, from the handle straight away from you.

- The stream should land on that line during the base, the pattern, and the cut-through.
- The most common aim error is pouring slightly towards yourself — the near edge of the cup feels like the centre from a standing position. Check from above, not from the side.
- A practical cue: line the spout up with the handle. If the stream is on the handle line, it is on the centre line.

The [heart guide](/learn/latte-art-heart) covers this for a single-centre pattern; the same line discipline is what keeps a [rosetta](/learn/latte-art-rosetta) straight.

## Cup handling: the quiet cause of lopsided pours

The cup is supposed to tilt towards the pitcher at the start, then level out as it fills. Two handling habits break symmetry:

1. **Rotating instead of tilting.** If the cup turns around its vertical axis while you pour, the pattern paints itself at an angle. Keep the handle pointing the same direction through the whole pour.
2. **Levelling unevenly.** Lowering one side of the cup before the other slides the surface sideways under the stream, and the design grows lopsided even with perfect aim.

Both are easier to feel than to see. If your designs twist, film one pour from the front — the rotation is usually obvious on video.

## Flow: check the stream itself

Sometimes the pitcher is the problem. A spout that drips or a pitcher held at a slight roll makes the stream fall in a curve, and wiggle patterns drift to whichever side the stream favours.

- Hold the pitcher so the spout points straight down at the start of the pattern.
- Keep the wrist level through the wiggle; the side-to-side motion should be parallel to the cup's surface, not scooped.
- If every pattern drifts the same direction regardless of cup or aim, practise pouring water into a sink and watch the stream — a persistent curve points to pouring posture, not the cup.

## Fix the pour you just made

| What showed up | Try this next |
| --- | --- |
| Pattern on one side of the cup | Pour onto the handle line, check from above |
| Pattern twisted at an angle | Stop rotating the cup mid-pour |
| One side bigger than the other | Level the cup evenly as it fills |
| Design slides sideways as it grows | Level the pitcher wrist; check the stream |
| Symmetric but off-centre heart | Start the push on the centre line, not where the base happened to be |
| Only wiggle patterns drift | Slow the drag and keep the wiggle parallel to the surface |

## A three-pour symmetry session

1. First pour: ignore the pattern. Pour the base onto the handle line and stop. Check from above.
2. Second pour: add the pattern, then photograph the cup from directly overhead.
3. Third pour: fix whichever of the three causes the photo showed.

Overhead photos make asymmetry impossible to un-see — and easy to track. Comparing this week's overhead shots to last month's is one of the clearest progress signals there is; the [practice routine](/learn/latte-art-practice-routine) builds that comparison in.
