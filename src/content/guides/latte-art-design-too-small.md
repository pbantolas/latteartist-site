Your latte art is small because the cup is nearly full when the design starts. The base phase — the high pour that mixes milk under the crema — is eating the room your pattern needs.

The fix is to start the low pour when the cup is half full, not three-quarters. Everything below is about finding that moment reliably.

## The room your design actually needs

A heart does not shrink to fit the space left. It forms at whatever size your flow creates, and if the surface is almost at the rim, there is nowhere for it to spread, so it either stays tiny or overflows.

One useful mental model is that the base and the design split the cup between them. A big base means a small design, every time.

## The half-full rule

Tilt the cup, pour high and steady into the centre, and watch the fill level. When the cup reaches roughly half full, drop the spout close and begin the push.

"Half full" is a starting point, not a law:

- **Small cups** (flat whites, cortados): start even earlier (see the [small-cup guide](/learn/flat-white-cortado-latte-art/)).
- **Wide bowls:** you have slightly more room; half to sixty percent works.
- **Multi-element patterns** like tulips: earlier still, because you need room for every layer.

## Other causes worth ruling out

Timing is the usual suspect, but check these before blaming it:

- **Flow too thin.** A timid trickle during the push draws a small design even with plenty of room. Increase the flow when you drop low. The push should feel decisive.
- **Milk too thick.** Dry foam does not spread; it sits where it lands as a lump. If the design is small *and* shapeless, check texture first with the [too thick or too thin guide](/learn/milk-too-thick-too-thin/).
- **Cut-through too early.** Cutting before the circle has grown locks in a small heart. Let the push finish.

The [troubleshooting guide](/learn/latte-art-troubleshooting/) covers the broader set of pour problems; this article goes deeper only on the timing question.

## An experiment to find your timing

Do this once, with three pours, changing nothing but when you drop low:

1. **Pour 1:** drop at one-third full. Expect a design with room to spare, possibly swimming in a thin base.
2. **Pour 2:** drop at half full. This is the target.
3. **Pour 3:** drop at two-thirds full, your current habit most likely. Expect the familiar tiny heart.

Photograph each from above, and note the fill level at the drop. Comparing the three photos tells you more than thirty pours on autopilot, because you will see the design size track the base size directly.

If the photos look similar and you cannot tell when you dropped, that is a sign to [film one pour from the side](/learn/film-latte-art-pours/); the drop moment is obvious on video and invisible in a still.

Save the three photos with their notes. In LatteArtist, keeping attempts side by side like this turns "my hearts are small" into "my drop is two seconds late," a problem you can fix on the next pour.
