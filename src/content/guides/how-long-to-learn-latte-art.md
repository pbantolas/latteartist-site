There is no honest universal timeline, because progress depends on how often you pour and whether each pour teaches you something. The milestones are what transfer, and they arrive in roughly the same order for everyone.

Expect weeks of daily practice to reach a consistent heart, and months before multi-element patterns feel repeatable. My own practice history is one data point. Your pace will differ, but the sequence should look familiar.

## Milestone 1: Glossy milk on demand

**What it looks like:** the pitcher contents shine like wet paint, pour in one continuous stream, and no dry cap sits on top.

Everything else waits on this. If your milk textures correctly three pours out of five, keep working here. Patterns poured on unreliable milk teach you nothing, because you cannot tell technique errors from texture errors. The [steaming guide](/learn/how-to-steam-milk-for-latte-art/) and [microfoam explainer](/learn/what-is-microfoam/) cover the target texture.

## Milestone 2: White appears on the surface

**What it looks like:** during the low pour, a visible white mark forms and stays, instead of sinking under the crema.

This means your height and flow are right, even if the shape is not. Many beginners pour for weeks without white appearing because they never get the spout close enough. Dropping lower, earlier, usually unlocks this in a single session.

## Milestone 3: A recognisable heart

**What it looks like:** someone else can name the pattern without being told.

The first real heart often arrives suddenly, then disappears for a week. That is normal; the movement is not yet consistent. Follow the [heart sequence](/learn/latte-art-heart/) and resist chasing bigger patterns until hearts show up most sessions.

## Milestone 4: Placement and symmetry

**What it looks like:** hearts sit centred, upright, and sized to the cup rather than drifting or shrinking.

This stage is quieter but takes longer than learning the shape. Reviewing photos helps enormously here (see [how to read a saved pour](/learn/review-latte-art-photos/)), because placement errors are easier to see from above than from behind the pitcher.

## Milestone 5: A wiggle you control

**What it looks like:** a [ripple heart](/learn/ripple-heart/) or early [rosetta](/learn/latte-art-rosetta/) with even spacing, where you decide the wiggle rate instead of the wiggle deciding for you.

This is the stage where progress feels slow again. The wiggle is a new motor skill layered onto everything before it, and it temporarily makes your hearts worse. That regression is part of the sequence.

## What actually speeds things up

- **Frequency beats volume.** One or two focused pours daily teaches more than fifteen on a Sunday.
- **One variable per pour.** Change drop height or flow, not both.
- **Review within minutes.** A photo and a one-line note while the pour is fresh is worth more than a gallery you never look at.
- **Cheap repetition.** [Practising without wasting coffee](/learn/how-to-practice-latte-art-without-wasting-coffee/) keeps the cost of high-frequency practice low.

## The habit that matters more than the timeline

The learners who improve fastest answer one question after every pour: "what will I do differently next time?"

Saving each attempt with a photo and a note (which is what LatteArtist is for) turns that question from a guess into a lookup. Read [what I learned from 30 practice pours](/learn/30-practice-pours/) for a documented example of a focused practice block.
