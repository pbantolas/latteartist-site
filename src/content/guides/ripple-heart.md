A ripple heart is a solid heart poured with a gentle side-to-side wiggle during the push, so concentric rings stack around the white circle before you cut through. It teaches the rosetta's wiggle without the rosetta's pull-back.

If you can pour a [centred solid heart](/learn/latte-art-heart/) most of the time, you are ready for this one.

## What the wiggle is

The wiggle is a small, even side-to-side movement of the pitcher, driven from the wrist, while the spout stays close to the surface. The stream should swing a few millimetres each way — not the whole pitcher travelling across the cup.

Two rules make it work:

1. **Flow stays constant.** The wiggle changes direction, not rate. If flow pulses with each swing, you get blobs instead of rings.
2. **The spout stays low.** Lift even slightly and the rings stop forming.

## Step by step

1. **Set the base.** Tilt the cup, pour high and steady into the centre until the cup is about half full, exactly as for a solid heart.
2. **Drop low and start the push.** Bring the spout close to the surface and increase flow until white appears.
3. **Start with a small wiggle.** Once the white circle is stable, begin a gentle wrist wiggle — two or three swings per second is plenty. Rings should start wrapping around the incoming stream.
4. **Hold your position.** Unlike a rosetta, you do not pull back. Stay in the centre and let the rings stack outward.
5. **Cut through.** Ease the flow, lift slightly, and pull a thin stream through the middle. The cut turns the ringed circle into a pointed heart with ripples on both lobes.

## What you should see at each stage

- **After step 3:** faint concentric lines, evenly spaced on both sides. Uneven spacing means uneven swing speed.
- **After step 4:** a ringed circle about the size you want the heart to be. It will not grow during the cut.
- **After step 5:** clean ripples on the upper two-thirds, a sharp point at the bottom.

## Common failures and their fixes

| What showed up | Likely cause | Try next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solid heart, no rings | Wiggle too small or too slow | Exaggerate the swing; speed will come later |
| Zigzag lines, not rings | Wiggle too wide and fast | Halve the swing width, keep the flow steady |
| Blobs between rings | Flow pulsing with the wiggle | Practise the wrist motion over water first |
| Washed-out rings | Spout lifted during the wiggle | Keep the spout almost touching the surface |
| Lopsided ripples | Cup tilted or spout off-centre | Check alignment before dropping low |

## A dry-run drill

Before your next coffee, fill the pitcher with water and practise the wiggle into an empty sink or jug: constant flow, small swings, thirty seconds. You are training the wrist separately from everything else, so that on a real pour the only new element is the milk.

Log your first ten ripple-heart attempts with a photo and one line on the wiggle ("too wide", "flow pulsed"). In LatteArtist, comparing those attempts side by side shows the wiggle settling down far faster than memory does.
